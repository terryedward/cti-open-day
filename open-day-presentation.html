Real World Work
A typical day as a SOC Analyst
07:30 — Shift start
Morning briefing & handover
Read the overnight shift report. Check what incidents are still open. Make coffee. ☕
08:00 — Alert triage
Review the SIEM dashboard
312 new alerts overnight. Sort by severity. Find the 3 that matter from the 309 false alarms.
10:30 — Investigation
Follow the suspicious login
Someone logged in from Russia at 3am. Was it a VPN? Was it the attacker? Trace the evidence.
14:00 — Incident response
Confirm the breach, take action
Block the IP. Disable the account. Notify the ISO. Document everything for legal.
16:00 — Write handover
Shift report for next analyst
Document what happened, what you did, what needs follow-up. Go home.

The Full Pathway
One qualification suite. Three levels. Global exams.
CTU's cybersecurity qualifications aren't a single certificate — they're a career ladder. Each rung is registered with SAQA and embeds the international vendor exam employers actually screen for.
NQF 5 · 1 year · Entry
CSA
Occupational Certificate: Cybersecurity Analyst · SAQA ID 118986
Foundational threat-monitoring and analysis skills. Feeds directly into Tier 1 SOC / junior IT security roles.
🌍 Embedded exam: CompTIA Security+
NQF 6 · 1 year · Today's focus
CSP
Advanced Occupational Certificate: Cybersecurity Practitioner · SAQA ID 122068
The qualification today is about. Investigates, mitigates, and reports on real cyber incidents. Feeds SOC Tier 2/3, Vulnerability Analyst, IT Security Officer roles.
🌍 Embedded exam: Microsoft SC-200 (Security Operations Analyst)
NQF 7 · 1 year · Progression
CIO
Advanced Occupational Diploma: Chief Information Officer (Cybersecurity) · SAQA ID 121968
Moves from hands-on technical work into cybersecurity governance and leadership — the path toward Information Security Manager and, eventually, CISO.
🎓 Bridges into ISACA CISM/CISA and postgraduate study
🌍 Why the international exams matter this much
A SAQA/QCTO qualification proves you met a national standard — it's what gets you NSFAS funding and formal recognition in South Africa. A CompTIA or Microsoft exam proves a specific, globally benchmarked skill — it's what a hiring manager in Cape Town, London, or Dubai searches for by name on a CV. Employers increasingly list these certifications as hard requirements, not preferences. Graduates who hold both the local qualification and the international exam are the ones who qualify for the remote UK/US/EU contracts mentioned earlier today — the qualification opens the door locally, the exam opens it internationally.
🤖 A curriculum built for where the field is actually going
Cybersecurity isn't standing still, and neither is how we teach it. AI is now on both sides of this fight — attackers use it to write more convincing phishing and automate attacks, while modern SIEM and XDR platforms use it to detect threats faster than any human could alone. And on the horizon, quantum computing threatens to eventually break the encryption the entire internet currently relies on — which is why post-quantum readiness is already becoming a live conversation in the security industry. CTU keeps this qualification's teaching grounded in that reality, not frozen in whatever cybersecurity looked like five years ago.
Ask: "Has anyone here already heard of CompTIA or Microsoft certifications?" — gauge existing awareness before explaining.
